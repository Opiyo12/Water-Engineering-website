How to Choose the Right Borehole Depth for Your Site in Uganda

Borehole depth is one of the most critical decisions in any water project. Drill too shallow and you risk dry wells; too deep and costs escalate unnecessarily.

Our engineers break down the key geological factors, seasonal water table variations, and site assessment techniques used across Uganda's diverse terrain — from the rocky ridges of Kabale to the deep alluvial plains of the north.

Key factors we assess before drilling:

  • Local geology — rock type, fracture zones, and aquifer depth
  • Seasonal water table fluctuations (wet vs. dry season measurements)
  • Nearby boreholes as reference depth guides
  • Intended usage — domestic (30–80m) vs. community/commercial (80–200m+)
  • Pump type and required yield per hour

A hydrogeological survey before drilling saves an average of 35% on project costs and dramatically reduces the risk of a failed borehole. Never skip the survey — it is the single most important investment in your water project.

— Baba Engineering Field Team, Kampala

Solar Pump vs. Diesel Pump: Real Cost Comparison for Ugandan Farmers

We ran the numbers for a 5-acre farm in Amuru over last year. Solar wins by a large margin when you factor in fuel, repairs, and downtime.

A diesel pump setup for a 5-acre farm costs approximately UGX 28–34 million including fuel and repairs. A comparable solar system costs UGX 18–22 million installed — with near-zero running costs after that.

Cost breakdown:

  • Diesel: ~UGX 4.2M/year fuel + UGX 800K/year repairs
  • Solar: Zero fuel cost, ~UGX 150K/year maintenance
  • Break-even: typically 18–24 months after installation

If you plan to farm for more than 2 years, solar is the clear winner financially and operationally for Uganda's climate and fuel supply situation.

Drip vs. Sprinkler Irrigation: Which Is Right for Your Ugandan Farm?

Crop type, terrain, and water source all determine which system delivers the best yield improvement across East African conditions.

Drip irrigation works best for row crops (tomatoes, onions, capsicum, beans) and situations where water is scarce. It delivers water directly to the root zone, reducing waste by up to 60% vs. sprinklers.

Sprinkler systems are better for lawns, pastures, leafy greens, and open fields where uniform coverage matters. They need higher pressure but cover ground faster.

Our recommendation for most smallholder farms: start with drip for high-value crops, add sprinklers for pasture. A hybrid system typically gives the best ROI within the first growing season.

How We Built a Pumping Station for 2000 People in Kitgum in Just 6 Weeks

From site survey to commissioning in 6 weeks — a behind-the-scenes look at how our team delivered a large-scale community water system.

The project involved drilling two boreholes to 120m depth, installing solar-powered submersibles with a 10,000L elevated storage tank, and laying 2.4km of HDPE distribution piping to 12 villages.

6-week timeline:

  • Week 1–2: Hydrogeological survey and borehole siting
  • Week 2–3: Drilling and casing (both boreholes)
  • Week 3–4: Tank foundation, elevated structure, pump installation
  • Week 4–6: Pipeline, connections, water quality testing, handover

The system now supplies over 3,000 residents at UGX 200 per 20L jerrycan — down from the previous vendor price of UGX 800–1,200.

5 Signs Your Water Tank Needs Urgent Cleaning or Replacement

Neglected tanks are a leading cause of waterborne disease in Uganda. Learn what our technicians look for when servicing residential and commercial tanks.

Our technicians have inspected over 800 tanks across Kampala and Central Uganda. The 5 most common warning signs:

  1. Discoloured water — brown, green or cloudy indicates algae, rust or sediment
  2. Unusual smell or taste — sulphur or organic smell signals contamination
  3. Visible cracks or leaks — even hairline cracks allow insects and debris entry
  4. Slimy interior walls — biofilm that can harbour E. coli and other bacteria
  5. Tank age over 10 years — plastic tanks degrade and leach compounds after a decade

We recommend professional cleaning every 6 months and a full inspection annually. Call us for a free tank assessment.
